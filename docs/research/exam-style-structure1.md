## 1. Assessment structure (2025 guide)

Source: guide, "Assessment outline" and "External assessment details" (verified).

| Component | HL | SL | Weighting (both) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 (1A + 1B, one sitting, two booklets, no break) | 2 h, 75 marks | 1 h 30 min, 55 marks | 36% |
| — Paper 1A: multiple choice | 40 MCQ, 40 marks (SL + AHL content) | 30 MCQ, 30 marks (SL content only) | |
| — Paper 1B: data-based + experimental-work questions | 35 marks | 25 marks | |
| Paper 2: short-answer and extended-response | 2 h 30 min, 90 marks | 1 h 30 min, 50 marks | 44% |
| Internal assessment (scientific investigation) | 24 marks, 10 h | same | 20% |

Total external time: HL 4 h 30 min, SL 3 h. Paper 3 and the options no longer exist.

**Calculator and data booklet (verified, and a change from the pre-2025 course):**
- Calculators are permitted in **every** paper, including Paper 1A. The May 2025 HL 1A and 1B cover pages both state that a calculator is required and that a clean copy of the chemistry data booklet is required.
- Consequence: MCQs can contain real arithmetic (non-round molar masses, percentage calculations, pH with decimals). The old "numbers chosen for mental arithmetic" style is no longer guaranteed — though many 2025 MCQs still use friendly numbers.
- Some third-party sites still say "no calculator in Paper 1A". That is wrong for this guide.
- Even with the booklet available, 2025 MCQ stems sometimes printed the needed relationship (e.g. the ideal gas equation, the calorimetry equation, an atom-economy expression) in the stem.

**Other verified features**
- No negative marking in 1A. Four options A–D. Timing split between 1A and 1B is the candidate's choice.
- All papers test AO1–AO3; nature-of-science understanding may be required.
- Paper 1B (May 2025 HL TZ1): 3 multi-part questions totalling 35, each built on one investigation, parts worth 1–4 marks. November 2025 HL had at least 4 questions. So expect **3–4 scenario questions of roughly 8–15 marks each**.
- Paper 2 (May 2025 HL): 8 questions (TZ1), all compulsory, each a themed chain mixing several topics; most parts 1–3 marks, occasional 4–5.
- Paper 2 questions are organised around a substance or context (an element, a compound family), not around a syllabus topic. Structure 1 items appear as individual parts inside such chains.

**Teaching-hour weight of this strand** (guide): 1.1 = 2 h, 1.2 = 2 h SL + 1 h AHL, 1.3 = 3 h SL + 3 h AHL, 1.4 = 7 h, 1.5 = 3 h → 21 of 180 HL content hours (≈12%).
Observed 1A share in 2025 HL papers: about 5–8 of 40 MCQs are Structure 1 (counting stoichiometry items that sit in Reactivity 2.1 pushes it higher). Structure 1 is over-represented in Paper 1B, because solution preparation, dilution, separation and gas measurements are the practical backbone.

## 2. Question archetypes by subtopic

Notation: **[SL]** = core, examinable at both levels; **[AHL]** = HL only. Tariffs are typical marks. "MS" = markscheme.

### 1.1 Particulate nature of matter [all SL; no AHL content]

| # | Archetype | Paper / marks | What the MS rewards |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1-A | Classify element / compound / mixture; homogeneous vs heterogeneous, often paired with "best separation method" in a two-column option grid | 1A, 1 | — |
| 1.1-B | Distinguish element from compound in words | P2, 2 | One mark per definition; idea of "one type of atom / same proton number" vs "different elements chemically bonded (fixed ratio)". Verified May 2025 HL P2. |
| 1.1-C | Give a *physical* property that differs between a mixture's components or between an element and its compound | P2, 1 | Must be physical; chemical properties and molar mass rejected. |
| 1.1-D | Design a separation sequence for a 3-component solid mixture (magnetic + soluble + insoluble) | 1B, 4 (max from ~5 points) | One mark per valid step in workable order: magnet; add water to dissolve; filter; dry residue; evaporate filtrate. Sieving / particle-size methods rejected. Verified May 2025 HL 1B. |
| 1.1-E | Name the technique for two miscible liquids and the property exploited | 1B, 2 | Technique (distillation) + property (boiling point), 1 each. |
| 1.1-F | Recrystallisation: order the steps, explain solvent choice, effect of too much / too little solvent on yield and purity | 1B, 1–3 per part | Key words carry marks ("hot" solvent, filter hot, cool, filter, rinse with cold solvent). Verified Nov 2025 HL 1B. |
| 1.1-G | Chromatography: identify a spot and calculate a retardation factor from a drawn chromatogram with a scale | 1A, 1 | Combines Rf arithmetic with polarity reasoning (which component travels further on a polar stationary phase). Verified in both May 2025 HL 1A papers. |
| 1.1-H | Name a change of state; interpret heating/cooling curve; kinetic-energy vs temperature; °C ↔ K | 1A, 1; P2, 1–2 | Kelvin as measure of average kinetic energy. |
| 1.1-I | State symbols in equations | embedded | Marked only when the question asks for them (then usually one mark for "correct species AND state symbols", one for balancing). |
| 1.1-J | Percentage composition of a separated mixture; explain why recovered percentages exceed 100%; why a visual hypothesis was wrong | 1B, 1–2 | Error + matching improvement (e.g. sample not dry → heat to constant mass). Density vs volume reasoning. |

Typical MCQ distractors: heterogeneous/homogeneous swapped; filtration offered for a solution; sublimation/deposition swapped; evaporation vs boiling; Rf inverted (solvent ÷ spot) or measured from the wrong line; wrong spot chosen because polarity logic reversed.

### 1.2 The nuclear atom [SL: 1.2.1–1.2.2; AHL: 1.2.3 mass spectra]

| # | Archetype | Paper / marks | What the MS rewards |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.2-A [SL] | Count p, n, e from nuclear notation, including ions; "which species have the same number of …" with unnamed symbols (letters not real elements) | 1A, 1 | Verified May 2025 HL 1A. |
| 1.2-B [SL] | Deflection of p, n, e beams in an electric field — match paths to particles | 1A, 1 | Direction AND relative size of deflection (electron bends far more). |
| 1.2-C [SL] | Write the nuclear symbol of an isotope | P2, 1 | Mass number top, atomic number bottom, correct symbol. |
| 1.2-D [SL] | Isotope physical vs chemical properties: which property is the same/different for isotopically substituted substances | 1A, 1 | Same chemistry/electron arrangement; different mass, density, rate of diffusion, mp/bp. |
| 1.2-E [SL] | Relative atomic mass from isotope masses and % abundances | P2, 2 | M1 weighted-sum expression; M2 answer. "Award [2] for correct final answer." Verified May 2025 TZ2 and Nov 2025 HL P2. |
| 1.2-F [SL] | Reverse: find the abundance of one of two isotopes from Ar (let x = fraction) | 1A, 1; P2, 2 | Algebra set-up, then value. |
| 1.2-G [AHL] | Read a mass spectrum (m/z vs relative abundance, bar chart) → identify isotopes, calculate Ar | P2, 2–3; 1A, 1 | Read peak heights; if abundances are not percentages, divide by the total. |
| 1.2-H [AHL] | Diatomic-molecule pattern: explain the number and relative heights of molecular-ion peaks for an element with two isotopes of near-equal abundance (1:2:1), or 3:1 isotopes (9:6:1) | P2, 2 (max) | Identify which isotope combination gives each m/z; the mixed combination can form two ways so is twice as probable. Verified Nov 2025 HL P2. |
| 1.2-I [AHL] | Identify the species responsible for a peak | P2, 1 | The positive charge is essential — MS states no mark if "+" is missing. (This overlaps Structure 3.2 fragmentation.) |

Notes for writers
- Operational details of the mass spectrometer are explicitly **not assessed**. Do not ask about ionisation/acceleration/deflection/detection stages.
- Specific isotopes need not be memorised; always supply the data.
- Verified MS trap: when the question's own isotope data give a value slightly different from the periodic-table value, the **data-booklet Ar is not accepted** for the answer mark. Write questions so the calculated answer differs visibly from the booklet value, and say so in the solution.
- Typical precision: answer to 2 decimal places (matching the booklet's Ar style) — but see §5; sig-fig penalties apply only when the MS says so.

Typical MCQ distractors: neutrons = mass number; electrons of a cation calculated by adding instead of subtracting charge; simple (unweighted) mean of isotope masses; abundances swapped; mass number confused with relative atomic mass; "isotopes have different chemical properties".

| # | Archetype | Paper / marks | What the MS rewards |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.3-A [SL] | Continuous vs line spectrum; relation between colour, wavelength, frequency, energy | 1A, 1; P2, 1 | Higher frequency = shorter wavelength = higher energy. |
| 1.3-B [SL] | Hydrogen emission spectrum: which transition gives UV / visible / IR (to n = 1 / 2 / 3); which transition has highest energy / shortest wavelength; draw arrows on an energy-level diagram | 1A, 1; P2, 1–2 | Arrow direction (downwards for emission) and correct levels. Series *names* (Lyman etc.) are not assessed — do not require them. |
| 1.3-C [SL] | Explain how a line spectrum arises / why it is evidence for discrete energy levels | P2, 2–3 | Electron excited to higher level; falls back emitting a photon; photon energy = difference between levels; fixed levels → specific frequencies. |
| 1.3-D [SL] | Convergence as evidence that levels get closer together at higher energy | 1A, 1; P2, 1 | Verified Nov 2025 HL 1A (choose the best piece of evidence). |
| 1.3-E [SL] | Maximum electrons in level n (2n²); number of orbitals in a sublevel/level | 1A, 1 | Verified May 2025 HL 1A. |
| 1.3-F [SL] | Shape/orientation of s and p orbitals — choose the correct pair of drawings | 1A, 1 | Sphere vs dumbbell; three p orientations. |
| 1.3-G [SL] | Full or condensed configuration of an atom or ion up to Z = 36 | P2, 1; 1A, 1 | Either full or [noble gas] form accepted; for atoms either order of 4s/3d accepted in the MS (verified Nov 2025: both orders listed). |
| 1.3-H [SL] | Transition-metal ion configuration (4s lost first) incl. Cr and Cu exceptions | 1A, 1; P2, 1 | Verified May 2025 HL (a d-block 2+ ion in P2; Cr²⁺ in 1A). |
| 1.3-I [SL] | Orbital (arrow-in-box) diagram: pick or draw the ground-state diagram | 1A, 1; P2, 1 | Hund's rule (singly occupied, parallel spins), Pauli (opposite spins in a pair), Aufbau. |
| 1.3-J [SL] | Combined grid: formula of an ionic compound + configuration of each ion | 1A, 1 | Links Structure 2.1. Verified May 2025 HL 1A. |
| 1.3-K [SL] | Deduce ion charge / group / number of unpaired electrons from a configuration | 1A, 1 | |
| 1.3-L [AHL] | First IE ↔ wavelength/frequency at the convergence limit. Both directions occur: (i) given λ or f of the limit, calculate IE in kJ mol⁻¹; (ii) given IE in kJ mol⁻¹ (from booklet), calculate the wavelength of the limit | P2, 3 | Three marking points: energy per atom (÷ or × N_A, with kJ↔J); frequency via E = hf; wavelength via c = λf. "Award [3] for correct final answer." Direction (ii) verified in **both** May 2025 HL P2 timezones — treat as a staple. Answer accepted in m or nm. |
| 1.3-M [AHL] | Name the feature of a spectrum that corresponds to ionisation | P2, 1 | "Limit of convergence" (high-energy limit accepted). |
| 1.3-N [AHL] | Explain the general trend in first IE across a period / down a group | 1A, 1; P2, 2 | Across: increasing nuclear charge, same shielding/same occupied levels, smaller radius. Down: outer electron further away / more shielding. |
| 1.3-O [AHL] | Explain a discontinuity: group 2→13 (s to p sublevel) and group 15→16 (paired electron repulsion) | P2, 2 | Must name the sublevels/orbitals and give the energetic reason (p higher in energy than s / shielded by s; paired electrons in one p orbital repel). Vague "stability of half-filled shell" answers are weak. |
| 1.3-P [AHL] | Compare first IE of two neighbouring elements (not necessarily period 2/3) | P2, 2 | Verified May 2025 TZ2: nuclear-charge + same-shielding argument credited; an argument about "leaving a complete subshell" explicitly rejected. |
| 1.3-Q [AHL] | Successive IE data (table or log-free graph of IE vs ionisation number) → deduce group; identify where the large jump is | 1A, 1; P2, 1–2 | Jump after the nth electron ⇒ n valence electrons ⇒ group. Verified May 2025 HL 1A (graph of ten successive IEs; options included groups 1, 2, 13, 14). |
| 1.3-R [AHL] | Explain why successive IEs increase, and why the big jump occurs | P2, 2 | Same nuclear charge attracting fewer electrons / less repulsion; jump = electron removed from an inner level closer to the nucleus with less shielding. |
| 1.3-S [AHL] | Write the equation for the first (or nth) IE with state symbols | P2, 1 | Gaseous atom → gaseous ion + e⁻; one mole of electrons removed. |
| 1.3-T [AHL link] | Link to Structure 3.1: why transition elements show variable oxidation states (successive IEs close in value) | 1A, 1 | Verified May 2025 HL 1A. |

Typical MCQ distractors
- Configurations: 4s electrons kept and 3d removed for cations; Cr written 4s²3d⁴, Cu 4s²3d⁹; anion drawn with the neutral atom's configuration; noble-gas core off by one period.
- Orbital diagrams: paired electrons before all p orbitals singly filled; parallel spins in one box; 2s left half-filled.
- 2n²: answers of n, 2n, 8 ("octet"), or the cumulative total up to n.
- Successive IE: counting the jump position one off (group = number *before* the jump); reading group 13 as "3"; picking the largest absolute gap on a non-log graph instead of the largest ratio.
- IE trend reasons: "shielding decreases across a period", "radius increases across a period", electronegativity offered as a cause rather than a parallel trend.
- Spectra: visible lines assigned to n = 1; absorption/emission arrow direction; "lines converge at low energy".

Unit handling in 1.3-L (the commonest AHL calculation here): kJ mol⁻¹ → J mol⁻¹ (×10³) → J per atom (÷ 6.02×10²³) → f = E/h → λ = c/f; nm ↔ m (×10⁻⁹). Each conversion is a separate place to lose a mark; ECF rescues later steps.

Notes for writers
- Theoretical/percentage yield and atom economy are syllabus-located in Reactivity 2.1, but 2025 papers freely blend them with 1.4. For a Structure-1-only bank, tag such items "links R2.1" and keep the 1.4 skill as the main demand.
- Back-titration, ppm and mole fraction are not in the Structure 1.4 statements — avoid as required knowledge. [UNVERIFIED whether they can appear with scaffolding]
- Relative atomic/formula mass have no units; molar mass has g mol⁻¹. A "state the unit" part is fair.

Typical MCQ distractors: forgetting diatomic molecules (atoms vs molecules); not multiplying by subscripts/ions per formula unit; empirical formula from % ÷ atomic number, or % ratio used directly without dividing by Ar; ratio not scaled to integers (stopping at 1 : 1.5); cm³ not converted to dm³ (answer ×1000); mole ratio inverted or ignored; choosing the reactant with the smaller mass or smaller amount as limiting without using the ratio; counting H₂O(l) as gas volume; adding rather than subtracting excess.

## 3. Common errors and misconceptions

Provenance: no official 2025+ subject report could be accessed. Items marked ✔MS are inferred directly from what 2025 markschemes explicitly reject or guard against. All others are **[UNVERIFIED — teacher commentary / long-standing examiner themes, not checked against a subject report]**.

**1.1**
- Offering a chemical property when a physical one is asked (✔MS), or quoting molar mass as a "property" (✔MS).
- Separation steps in an unworkable order; forgetting to dry or to evaporate; "sieve" for particles of similar size (✔MS).
- Solution-preparation descriptions omitting distilled/deionised water (✔MS) or mixing; using a measuring cylinder for accurate volumes (✔MS).
- Confusing homogeneous mixture with compound; thinking a solution can be filtered.
- Rf > 1, or measuring from the bottom of the paper instead of the baseline.
- Temperature *differences* converted by adding 273 (ΔT is the same in °C and K).

**1.2**
- Quoting the periodic-table Ar instead of calculating from the isotope data given (✔MS).
- Mass number vs relative atomic mass vs molar mass used interchangeably; giving Ar a unit.
- Dropping the positive charge on species detected in a mass spectrometer (✔MS).
- Not normalising when abundances do not add to 100.
- Rounding intermediate values so the final Ar drifts in the second decimal place.
- For diatomic spectra: missing that the mixed-isotope molecule is twice as likely.
- Saying isotopes differ in electrons or chemical properties.

**1.3**
- Removing 3d before 4s when forming d-block cations; writing Cr and Cu without the exceptions; Cu⁺/Cu²⁺ and Cr³⁺ built from the wrong atom configuration.
- Explaining IE discontinuities with "half-filled/full sublevels are stable" and no mechanism; a "complete subshell left behind" argument was explicitly refused in May 2025 (✔MS).
- IE trend: saying shielding changes across a period; omitting that occupied levels are the same.
- Using the term "ionisation energy" loosely for other energy changes (✔MS note in a Born–Haber context).
- IE ↔ λ calculation: forgetting kJ → J; forgetting the per-mole ↔ per-atom step (answer wrong by 6.02×10²³); using λ in nm inside c = λf; inverting E = hf.
- Mixing up continuous and line spectra; emission vs absorption; thinking the visible lines end at n = 1.
- Confusing "energy levels converge" with "lines converge": the lines get closer at **high** energy / high frequency / short wavelength.
- Successive IE: off-by-one group deduction; not recognising that a period 3+ element in group 13–18 needs the group number, not the number of valence electrons ("3" vs "13").
- Orbital vs sublevel vs energy level used as synonyms; "orbit" for orbital.

**1.4**
- cm³ not converted to dm³ in n = cV (✔MS working always shows the ×10⁻³).
- Naming the limiting reactant without showing both amounts (✔MS: no credit).
- Combustion analysis: forgetting H is 2 × n(H₂O); forgetting to find O by difference; rounding ratios too early (e.g. 2.66 → 3 instead of ×3).
- Empirical vs molecular formula confusion; not simplifying, or simplifying a molecular formula when asked for molecular.
- Using integer Ar values instead of the booklet's 2 d.p. values (usually harmless, occasionally shifts the last sig fig).
- Atoms vs molecules vs ions when applying N_A.
- Dilution described as "add X cm³ of water" rather than "make up to the mark".
- g dm⁻³ and mol dm⁻³ confused.

**1.5**
- Units in pV = nRT: mixing kPa with m³, or Pa with dm³; cm³ → m³ using 10⁻³ instead of 10⁻⁶ (✔MS: conversion is its own marking point).
- Not converting °C to K (✔MS: a named [2 max] penalty value appears for using °C in a Nov 2025 calculation in another topic — the same logic applies here).
- Using 22.4 instead of 22.7 dm³ mol⁻¹; applying molar volume at non-STP conditions.
- Real gases: reversing the conditions; saying "particles have no volume/forces" for a real gas; attributing deviation to "collisions not elastic" only.
- Graphs: straight line for p vs V; V vs T(°C) drawn through the origin.
- Percentage language: a halving described as a 100% decrease.

**Cross-cutting**
- Hidden working: with ECF available, unshown working forfeits recoverable marks.
- "Describe" answered with reasons, "explain" answered without them.
- Final answers with no unit when the question says "state the units" / "giving the correct units" (✔MS: unit carries its own mark in such parts).

## 5. Significant figures, rounding, units — markscheme conventions

Verified from the general instructions printed at the front of 2025 HL markschemes, plus observed practice.

1. **Default rule: no penalty for units or significant figures unless the Notes column for that part says so.** Practice banks should therefore not deduct for s.f. by default — but should *teach* the right habit in the worked solution.
2. **When a unit is asked for, it is a separate mark** (e.g. a 2-mark "estimate … giving the correct units" part: value 1, unit 1).
3. **Correct final answer earns full marks** ("Award [n] for correct final answer"), working or not — *except* where the MS says working must be seen (limiting-reactant style justifications).
4. **ECF (error carried forward):** an error is penalised once; later marking points using the wrong value correctly are awarded. MS sometimes list the ECF value for a common slip. Named partial-credit outcomes also appear ("Award [2 max] for …" a specific wrong value arising from one identified slip such as a sign error or Celsius temperature).
5. **Model answers in MS are given to the precision of the data**, usually 3 s.f. (4 s.f. when the data are 4 s.f.). Scientific notation for very small/large values. Ranges are accepted for graph readings and estimates.
6. Alternatives: "/" separates acceptable words; "OR" separates acceptable answers; "ALTERNATIVE 1/2" gives parallel full routes (e.g. working via moles or via masses). «…» encloses words not required. Underlined words are essential. "OWTTE" = equivalent wording accepted. "max" = more marking points listed than marks available. The "list principle" (LP) penalises a correct answer accompanied by an incorrect one in a list.
7. Equations: balanced symbol equation expected; state symbols ignored unless asked; name ≠ formula when one is specifically requested.
8. Candidates writing in a second language: meaning over grammar.
9. Unit conventions used in papers: negative-index form (mol dm⁻³, g mol⁻¹, kJ mol⁻¹, J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹); volumes in cm³ or dm³ in solution work and m³ (or dm³ with kPa) in gas-equation work; temperature in °C in stems and K in calculations; SI only for pressure and volume in the gas equation (guide) — so no atm, mmHg, L or mL.
10. Uncertainties (1B): absolute uncertainty to 1 s.f., value quoted to the same decimal place; percentage uncertainties added for × and ÷; answers within a stated range accepted.

Constants and relationships available in the data booklet (do not expect recall; do expect selection): n = m/M, n = CV, PV = nRT, combined gas law, E = hf, c = λf; N_A = 6.02×10²³ mol⁻¹; R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; molar volume at STP = 22.7 dm³ mol⁻¹ (2.27×10⁻² m³ mol⁻¹), STP = 273.15 K and 100 kPa; h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J s; c = 3.00×10⁸ m s⁻¹; Ar values to 2 d.p.; first ionisation energies; EM spectrum; colour wheel (cited as section 15 in a May 2025 paper). Guide-verified that these relationships/values are "given in the data booklet"; exact section numbers other than 15 are **[UNVERIFIED]** — writers should say "use the data booklet" rather than cite a section number unless checked.

Recommended house rules for the practice bank
- Give data to 3 s.f. (4 for standard-solution work); model answers to the least precise datum; accept ±1 in the last figure.
- Carry unrounded values through multi-step work; round once at the end.
- Flag in solutions which step earns each mark (M1, M2 …), where ECF would apply, and which answers are "accept"/"do not accept".

## 6. Blueprint for the practice bank

### 6.1 Tiers
- **Foundation check** — single idea, one step, familiar wording. Mostly MCQ and 1–2 mark "state/calculate". Purpose: confirm recall and basic procedures.
- **Exam standard** — matches the archetypes and tariffs in §2: 2–4 mark structured parts, MCQs with reasoned distractors, 1B-style data handling.
- **Challenge (level 7 stretch, within syllabus)** — multi-step chains, unfamiliar contexts, data-based evaluation, cross-topic links. No new content.

### 6.2 Suggested numbers (≈180 items; scale proportionally)

| Subtopic | Foundation | Exam standard | Challenge | Total | Suggested format mix (MCQ : structured : data-based) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Particulate nature | 8 | 10 | 4 | 22 | 45 : 30 : 25 |
| 1.2 Nuclear atom (+ AHL mass spectra) | 8 | 12 | 6 | 26 | 45 : 45 : 10 |
| 1.3 Electron configurations (+ AHL IE) | 14 | 22 | 12 | 48 | 40 : 50 : 10 |
| 1.4 The mole | 14 | 24 | 12 | 50 | 35 : 40 : 25 |
| 1.5 Ideal gases | 8 | 14 | 8 | 30 | 40 : 40 : 20 |
| Mixed Structure 1 chains (Paper 2 style, 8–12 marks each) | — | 3 | 3 | 6 | structured |
| **Total** | **52 (≈29%)** | **85 (≈47%)** | **45 (≈25%)** | **182** | |

Weighting rationale: 1.4 has a third of the teaching hours and feeds every later calculation; 1.3 carries most of the AHL content; 1.1 and 1.5 are small but reliably examined (1.1 especially in Paper 1B).

AHL coverage minimums: mass-spectrum Ar (≥4), diatomic isotope patterns (≥2), IE ↔ convergence limit in both directions (≥6), IE trend/discontinuity explanations (≥6), successive IE table and graph (≥6).

Tagging per item: subtopic + syllabus statement (e.g. S1.3.6), SL/AHL, tier, paper style (1A/1B/2), command term, marks, skills (unit conversion, graph reading, ECF chain), links to other topics.

### 6.3 Authoring rules
- MCQ: exactly 4 options; every distractor produced by a named, realistic error (record it in the explanation); no "all/none of the above"; grids (two or three columns of paired answers) are common and welcome; calculator arithmetic allowed but keep to ≤2 minutes.
- Structured: stem context → parts (a), (b)(i)… with marks in square brackets; later parts may depend on earlier answers (state ECF in the solution).
- Data-based: give a table or described graph with units and uncertainties; ask for processing, a graph skill, an error source + improvement, and a judgement on a hypothesis.
- Markscheme per item: marking points one per mark, accepted alternatives, common wrong answer with its diagnosis, ECF note.
- Use data-booklet constants exactly as listed in §5.
- Elements up to Z = 36 only for configurations; isotope data supplied; SI units for gases.

### 6.4 Making it hard without leaving the syllabus
1. **Chain skills across subtopics.** Combustion data → empirical formula → gas data (pV = nRT) → molar mass → molecular formula. One slip tests ECF resilience. (This exact *type* of chain is verified exam practice.)
2. **Reverse the usual direction.** Give Ar and ask for the abundance; give IE and ask for the convergence wavelength; give the final diluted concentration and ask for the stock volume; give the gas density and ask for M.
3. **Unfamiliar element or context, familiar principle.** IE comparisons for period 4 p-block or heavier neighbours argued from nuclear charge/shielding; mass spectrum of an element students have not met; an industrial or environmental gas sample.
4. **Stack unit conversions.** nm, kJ mol⁻¹, cm³, kPa and °C in one problem; ask for the answer in a specified unit.
5. **Data that resist the pattern.** Successive IEs for an element with two noticeable jumps (ask for group *and* period reasoning); mass spectrum with abundances as peak heights that do not sum to 100; isotope pattern for X₂ with 3:1 isotopes.
6. **Proportional reasoning without numbers.** "Pressure doubled and absolute temperature raised by 50% — by what factor does the volume change?"; compare numbers of atoms in equal masses of two compounds.
7. **Evaluate rather than calculate.** Which error makes a measured molar volume too high? Does heating to constant mass matter? Why does % uncertainty grow on dilution? Which glassware choice changes the answer?
8. **Explain-with-evidence items.** Use supplied IE values to *justify* a sublevel model; use a described spectrum to argue energy levels converge.
9. **Limiting reagent hidden inside another task.** Gas volume produced when both solution and solid amounts are given; excess gas remaining after reaction of gas mixtures.
10. **Link questions (the guide's "linking questions").** IMF strength ↔ real-gas deviation (S2.2); successive IEs ↔ variable oxidation states (S3.1); isotopes ↔ mass-spectrum molecular-ion patterns (S3.2); configurations ↔ ion formulas and periodic trends (S2.1, S3.1); mole ↔ yield and atom economy (R2.1). Keep the Structure 1 skill dominant and tag the link.

Do **not** raise difficulty with: Rydberg equation or named spectral series; quantum numbers; mass-spectrometer operation; van der Waals equation or compressibility calculations; non-SI gas units; configurations beyond Z = 36; partial pressures/Dalton's law, Graham's law, mole fraction, ppm [last four: UNVERIFIED as out of scope — they are not in the Structure 1 statements read].
