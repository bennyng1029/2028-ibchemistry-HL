Notes for writers
- Polyatomic ions to be known by name and formula (guide): ammonium, hydroxide, nitrate, hydrogencarbonate, carbonate, sulfate, phosphate. Others (nitrite, sulfite, chlorate, etc.) must be given or be deducible from an oxidation-number name.
- Guide 2.1.1 includes ions of different charges from a transition element at SL. Explaining *why* (successive IEs close in value) and writing d-block ion configurations are AHL (3.1.9).
- Lattice enthalpy is treated qualitatively here: "influenced by ion radius and charge". No Born–Landé or Kapustinskii calculations.
- "Charge density" appears in MS answers as an acceptable way to combine charge and radius.

Typical MCQ distractors: subscripts swapped (crossing charges the wrong way); sulfite/sulfate or nitrite/nitrate confused; brackets omitted for two or more polyatomic ions; configurations of neutral atoms offered for the ions; "ionic compounds conduct when solid"; "electrons carry the current in molten salts"; larger ions said to give stronger lattices; "molecules of NaCl"; "ionic lattice held by covalent bonds".

Notes for writers
- SL Lewis formulas: at most four electron pairs on any atom, incomplete octets included; SL VSEPR: at most four domains. Five and six domains, formal charge, resonance, σ/π and hybridization are AHL. Tag accordingly even when the molecule is simple.
- Guide: bond dipoles may be shown with partial charges or vectors. "Van der Waals forces" is an inclusive term for London, dipole–induced dipole and dipole–dipole forces (not hydrogen bonding).
- Guide: locating agents not required; operating details of GC and HPLC not assessed.
- A double bond counts as one electron domain; the guide asks for the effect of lone pairs *and multiple bonds* on bond angles.
- Molecular orbital theory, sp³d / sp³d² hybridization, and dipole-moment calculations are outside the guide.
- For AHL species with expanded octets, MS accept more than one Lewis formula where formal-charge and octet versions both exist (May 2025 TZ3 accepted an alternative for a period 3 oxide, "with the formal charges" shown). Write markschemes that accept both unless the question is specifically about formal charge.

Typical MCQ distractors
- Shapes: electron domain geometry given as the molecular geometry (and the reverse); "tetrahedral" for any AX₄ regardless of lone pairs; "trigonal planar" for AX₃ with a lone pair; "linear" for bent triatomics; seesaw / square planar / tetrahedral confused.
- Angles: 109.5° for all four-domain species; 120° for trigonal pyramidal; 90° in tetrahedral drawings; 180° for water.
- Polarity: polar bonds ⇒ polar molecule (symmetrical molecules offered); the largest electronegativity difference chosen without checking geometry.
- Intermolecular forces: hydrogen bonding claimed for any molecule containing H and an electronegative atom somewhere (H not attached to N/O/F); "dipole–dipole" for a hydrogen-bonded hydride; covalent bonds "broken on boiling"; higher molar mass chosen without regard to branching; the most branched isomer chosen as highest boiling.
- R_F: solvent ÷ spot; distances measured from the bottom edge instead of the baseline; partial distances added wrongly; polarity logic reversed.
- Formal charge: oxidation state given instead; lone-pair electrons counted once each *and* bonding electrons counted fully; sign reversed.
- σ/π: every multiple bond counted as all π; C–H bonds forgotten in a condensed or skeletal formula; triple bond counted as one π.
- Hybridization: sp³ for every carbon; domains miscounted on atoms carrying lone pairs; sp for a doubly bonded carbon.
- Benzene: "unsaturated" or "can be hydrogenated" offered as evidence against the Kekulé structure (both are consistent with it).

Notes for writers
- The triangle uses Δχ (vertical) and mean χ (horizontal) from booklet electronegativities. Mirrored 2025 papers cite section 9 for electronegativity and section 17 for the triangle. A static site cannot assume the student has the booklet open, so supply the electronegativities, and for "read the triangle" items supply either a simplified inline SVG triangle or the region boundaries in words.
- Electronegativity values seen in MS working (booklet values, to 1 d.p.): O 3.4, P 2.2, Br 3.0, Ca 1.0; the Fe/S pair gave Δχ 0.8 and mean 2.2. Other values **[UNVERIFIED]** — writers should state the values they use in the stem.
- Monomer structures do not have to be learned: provide them or make them deducible from the polymer (guide).

## 3. Markscheme conventions that apply to these topics

All from mirrored 2025 HL markschemes unless marked as guide. General conventions (ECF, list principle, OWTTE, "max") are in the Structure 1 file §5.

**Lewis formulas**
- Electron pairs as dots, crosses, lines or any mixture (guide and MS).
- All valence electrons shown: every lone pair on every atom, terminal atoms included. One mark, all or nothing.
- Ions: the charge must be shown; square brackets are not required. Formal charges on atoms in place of an overall charge were accepted.
- A delocalised drawing is not a Lewis formula. One resonance form is enough unless resonance structures are asked for.
- Where both an expanded-octet and an octet-with-formal-charges version exist, both were accepted.
- Coordination bonds: an arrow or any clear label; the mark is for identifying the bond.

**Shapes and angles**
- Electron domain geometry and molecular geometry are separate marks; ECF from the candidate's own Lewis formula.
- Accepted synonyms: bent / V-shaped / angular; trigonal / triangular planar; trigonal pyramid(al); trigonal bipyramid(al).
- Bond angles below an ideal value are accepted as a range (100–107° for one trigonal pyramidal molecule). House rule for this bank: accept the ideal angle ±0.5° where there are no lone pairs; where lone pairs or multiple bonds distort the angle, accept "less than X" or a stated range and give the measured value in the solution.
- Explanations cite the number of electron domains, or the numbers of bonding and non-bonding pairs.

**Polarity and electronegativity**
- Bond polarity: "difference in electronegativity"; partial charges on both atoms for the mark.
- Molecular polarity: dipoles do not cancel / net dipole / asymmetric distribution of charge; tied to geometry.
- Bonding triangle: quote **both** the difference and the average; then the region. A percentage read from the triangle is accepted over a 10-point window.

**Intermolecular forces**
- Names: "London (dispersion) forces" is the expected term; "dispersion", "instantaneous / induced dipole" accepted; "LDF" appears in MS shorthand. "Van der Waals" is not accepted where London forces specifically are meant (guide: it is the inclusive term).
- Explanation marks: (1) identify the forces in each substance; (2) compare their strength; (3) where relevant, link to energy needed to separate molecules. Longer chain: more electrons / larger electron cloud / larger molar mass / larger surface area.
- Hydrogen bond: two-part description (H bonded to N, O or F; attraction to N, O or F on a neighbouring molecule).
- Network vs molecular comparisons must name what is broken in each.

**Bonding definitions**
- "Electrostatic attraction" is the key phrase for ionic, covalent and metallic bonds alike.
- Lattice enthalpy comparisons: ionic charge and ionic radius, or charge density.

**Periodicity**
- Oxidation states: sign first (+2), Roman numerals accepted; "2+" and "2" rejected.
- Trend explanations: nuclear charge (or effective nuclear charge / number of protons) plus shielding or occupied energy levels; radius alone is not enough.
- Configurations: full or condensed; either order of 4s and 3d for atoms.
- "Predict, with a reason" items can credit opposite conclusions if the reasoning is periodic and sound.

**Organic structures and names**
- "Any correct structural representation including skeletal" unless a type is specified. A specified full structural formula must show all bonds.
- Clearly incorrect bond connectivity is penalised once per paper.
- Names: hyphen and comma slips, locant order and alphabetical order were not penalised in 2025 MS. The bank should still model strict IUPAC form in solutions: lowest locants, alphabetical substituents, commas between numbers, hyphens between numbers and letters.
- Functional group names vs class names are not interchangeable (see 3.2-B).
- Repeating units: continuation bonds required; brackets and n optional; exactly one repeating unit.
- Enantiomers: wedge–dash with tetrahedral angles; chiral carbon marked by an asterisk or any clear indication.

**Spectra**
- IR: wavenumber range from the booklet AND the bond; any value within the range accepted; bond in symbols or words.
- ¹H NMR: number of signals; ratio in any order; splitting as a word or a number of peaks; shift as the booklet range.
- MS: positive charge essential on any ion; "molecular ion" for the highest m/z peak of the intact molecule.
- Colour: colour name, wavelength band or a wavelength inside the band are interchangeable.

**Chromatography**
- R_F = distance moved by the spot ÷ distance moved by the solvent front, both from the baseline; no unit; 2–3 s.f.; a tolerance window is given because distances are measured from a diagram.

### 4.2 Formats that work in a static web quiz for structural topics

1. **Shape/angle picker (`mcq`).** Stem gives a formula; options are two-column pairs (electron domain geometry / molecular geometry) or shape + angle. This is the IB grid format.
2. **Described-option Lewis structures (`mcq`).** Each option describes a structure in words: central atom, bond orders to each neighbour, lone pairs on each atom, charge. No drawing needed; distractors are missing lone pairs, wrong bond order, expanded vs octet versions.
3. **Small inline SVG options (`mcq`).** Four compact SVG Lewis or skeletal structures as options. Keep each under about 120 px wide so four fit on a phone; give every SVG a text alternative that does not reveal the answer.
4. **Count-and-type (`numeric`).** σ bonds, π bonds, lone pairs, electron domains, chiral centres, NMR signals. Two-part versions can be two linked items.
5. **Tick all that apply (`multi`).** Which species are polar; which have hydrogen bonding between their own molecules; which obey the octet rule; which conduct; which show cis–trans isomerism; which are coloured; which statements about a complex are true. This is the IB I–II–III format without the fixed combinations.
6. **Rank (`order`).** Boiling points; bond angles; ionic radii; lattice strength; metallic bond strength; bond length; acidity of oxides. State the direction in the stem ("lowest first").
7. **Sequence (`order`).** Steps to deduce a shape; steps of a chromatography run; steps from spectra to a structure; steps to name a compound.
8. **Name from a skeletal SVG (`mcq` or `short`).** MCQ distractors built from real naming errors (wrong chain, wrong numbering direction, branch absorbed into chain). `short` version with a markscheme tick-list: parent chain, suffix, locants, substituents.
9. **Match spectrum feature to group (`mcq` / `multi`).** A table of peaks (wavenumber, description) or a simplified SVG stick spectrum; options are bonds or compounds. Supply the relevant booklet rows in the stem.
10. **NMR table → structure (`mcq`).** Signals listed as shift / integration / splitting; four candidate isomers as options. Reverse version: structure given, pick the correct table.
11. **Fragment arithmetic (`numeric` / `mcq`).** Molecular ion and one peak given; find the mass lost and the group lost, or the m/z expected after losing a named group.
12. **Bonding-triangle locate (`numeric` then `mcq`).** Two numeric items for Δχ and mean χ, then an MCQ for the region using a simplified SVG triangle.
13. **Explain items (`short`).** Model answer plus a markscheme tick-list that mirrors IB marking points; include a "do not accept" line in the solution (for example "van der Waals" for London forces).
14. **Polymer builder (`mcq`).** Monomer SVG given, four repeating-unit SVGs as options; or the reverse.
15. **Data-table identification (`mcq`).** Melting point and conductivity (solid / molten / aqueous) for four unknowns; identify the structure type of each.
16. **Chromatogram reading (`numeric`).** An SVG chromatogram with a ruler; redundant distances included as in the 2025 MCQs.
17. **Wedge–dash recognition (`mcq`).** Four drawings: which pair are enantiomers, or which drawing is the mirror image of a given one.

Accessibility and marking notes: every SVG needs a neutral text alternative; do not encode the answer in colour alone; for `short` items list one marking point per mark and add accepted synonyms in the solution.

### 4.3 Ten ways to make challenge questions hard without leaving the syllabus

1. **Unfamiliar species, familiar rules.** Shapes, formal charges and hybridization for ions and interhalogen-type species students have not met; names for branched skeletal structures with the longest chain drawn bent; oxidation states in complex salts with counter-ions.
2. **Compete two factors.** Boiling points where molar mass and polarity pull in opposite directions; lattice strength where charge and radius disagree; branching versus chain length; hydrogen bonding versus a much larger London contribution.
3. **Chain the standard sequence and keep ECF alive.** Lewis formula → domains → shape → angle → polarity → intermolecular forces → a physical property, each part depending on the last.
4. **Reverse the direction.** Give the shape and angle and ask for the number of lone pairs; give the NMR table and ask for the structure; give the repeating unit and ask for the monomers; give the absorbed frequency and ask for the observed colour; give R_F and solvent distance and ask for the spot position.
5. **Ask for evidence, not the fact.** Which observation distinguishes delocalised from alternating bonding; which data show energy sublevels exist; which spectrum feature rules out an isomer.
6. **Discriminate between isomers with the minimum data.** Three or four isomers and one technique: which single measurement separates them, and what would be seen for each.
7. **Count under symmetry.** NMR signals and chiral centres in molecules with mirror planes or equivalent branches; number of structural or stereoisomers for a formula (state clearly what counts).
8. **Compare models.** Where the octet rule, the formal-charge criterion and VSEPR give different pictures of one species; where a compound sits on the bonding triangle versus what "metal + non-metal" predicts; the limits of the VSEPR model.
9. **Link across topics inside the syllabus.** Ligand as Lewis base (R3.4); colour ↔ ΔE with E = hf (S1.3); real-gas deviation ↔ intermolecular forces (S1.5); atom economy of polymerisation (R2.1); oxide character ↔ bonding type (2.4 with 3.1.5); IR activity ↔ molecular dipole (2.2.6).
10. **Data that mislead the careless.** Chromatograms with extra distances; mass spectra where the base peak is not the molecular ion; IR spectra with a broad acid O–H overlapping C–H; electronegativity pairs near a triangle boundary; property tables where one substance is a covalent network that conducts.

Do **not** raise difficulty with: molecular orbital theory; sp³d or sp³d² hybridization; E/Z nomenclature; R/S assignment; splitting patterns of d orbitals by geometry; recalled spectrochemical series; types of magnetism; naming of complex ions; ¹³C NMR; multiplets beyond quartets as a required answer; GC/HPLC instrument details; locating agents; percentage ionic character formulas; lattice-energy equations; parent chains longer than six carbons; compounds with two different types of functional group as a *naming* task; specific alloy compositions; recalled monomer structures.
